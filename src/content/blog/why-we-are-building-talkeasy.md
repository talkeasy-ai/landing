My grandmother, Emaye, is 76. She moved from Ethiopia to Alexandria, Virginia, nineteen years ago to be near her son. She doesn't speak or read English. But she learned the bus routes, built a life in a new country, and loves WhatsApp.

Her phone is the one thing that beats her.

## When the phone gets in the way

One wrong tap mutes it, and she can't call her children. Her medication reminder pops up, and she dismisses it because she can't read it fast enough. An app updates, her favourite show disappears behind a new screen, and she waits alone until a grandchild can come and fix it.

The device that was meant to connect her ends up isolating her.

## It's the screen, not her

None of this is because Emaye isn't capable. Technology was designed for people like the engineers who built it: people who read English, are always online, and grew up tapping glass.

So we asked a simple question: **what if all she had to do was speak?**

## What Talkeasy does

- **You talk, the phone listens.** Say "Call my daughter" in your own language and accent.
- **Nothing to read.** No menus, no passwords, no small print.
- **No internet needed.** Talkeasy works on the phone itself.
- **Your words stay with you.** What you say stays on your device.
- **Open source.** We build in public, so anyone can see how it works.

## Who it's for

Emaye is who we build it for. She stands for millions of people who are fully capable but have been shut out by a screen. She just wants to call her daughter. That shouldn't be too much to ask.
